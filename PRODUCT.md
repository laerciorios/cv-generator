# Product

## Register

product

## Users

General public: anyone who needs to build or update a resume, from developers
to non-technical job seekers. They arrive with a concrete, often stressful job
to do (apply for a position, update an outdated CV) and want to leave with a
polished PDF/DOCX as fast as possible. Sessions are focused work sessions on
desktop, with occasional mobile touch-ups. The app is fully client-side: no
account, no onboarding wall, data lives in the browser.

## Product Purpose

A single-page CV builder with live preview and client-side export (PDF, DOCX,
LaTeX, JSON). Success is a user filling in their data, seeing exactly what the
recruiter will see, and downloading an ATS-friendly document without friction,
sign-ups, or surprises. The preview IS the product's promise: what you see is
what exports.

## Brand Personality

Precise, focused, trustworthy. The tool feels like a professional instrument
(in the spirit of Linear or Raycast): dense but never cramped, fast, typographically
confident, zero ornament. The interface stays quiet so the user's document is
the loudest thing on screen.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS template look: identical card grids, purple gradients, hero
  metrics, glassy panels.
- Dated corporate internal tools: unintentional grays, cramped unstyled forms.
- Decoration for its own sake: glassmorphism, heavy shadows, attention-seeking
  animation.

## Design Principles

1. **The document is the hero.** The editor chrome recedes; the A4 preview is
   the visual anchor of every screen.
2. **Practice what you preach.** A tool that produces clean, well-typeset CVs
   must itself be cleanly typeset. Typography quality is the brand.
3. **Every control earns its pixel.** Dense, purposeful UI over airy filler.
   No wrapper for the sake of a wrapper.
4. **Zero-friction trust.** No accounts and local-only data are features:
   communicate save state and data ownership clearly and calmly.
5. **Speed reads as quality.** Instant feedback between typing and preview;
   motion only where it explains a state change.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA: contrast ratios, visible focus states, full keyboard operability,
labels on all controls, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected. Localized UI
(pt-BR, en, es) must never rely on color alone to convey state.
